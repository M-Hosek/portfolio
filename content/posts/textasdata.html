Text as Data: Digital Methods

27 October 2026

Course Description

The rise of generative AI has drawn the world’s attention to an important fact: data are not only numbers. Texts, languages, images and other cultural artifacts increasingly rendered in digital form, creating new possibilities and challenges for humanistic research. How can digital and computational methods help us ask new questions about textual and cultural materials? What do these methods reveal, and what do they obscure?

This course introduces students to digital approaches for studying texts, languages, and cultural materials from specific world regions. Organized around a rotating special topic, the course combines close reading, historical contextualization, and comparative analysis with hands-on work in Digital Humanities methods such as computational text analysis, mapping, and network analysis. Students will learn to prepare and interpret cultural data, with attention to the challenges of multilingual materials, translation, archives, and non-dominant writing systems. Rather than treating digital tools as replacements for humanistic interpretation, the course asks how computational methods can extend, complicate, and challenge our understanding of literature, history, politics, and culture. No prior technical experience is required.
