Eco-Perspectives from China

Last updated 19 August 2026

This is an original syllabus inspired by my dissertation research. It is designed as an upper-division seminar.

Course Description

Culture and nature are not opposites of each other, but rather are deeply interconnected. In this course, we study how Chinese concepts and narratives about nature are relevant to a wide range of topics both social (politics, morality, aesthetics) and material (resource extraction, climate change, non-human wellbeing). We discuss how perceptions of ecology are dynamic and multifaceted, not permanent. We will also debate whether artistic and literary expression are necessary to contemporary environmental activism. Students will walk away from this class with an appreciation for the diversity and revolutionary potential of Chinese eco-perspectives.

Course Itinerary

Week 1: What Does Literature have to Do with the Environment?

  • Raymond Williams, "Nature", from Keywords
  • Shi Haiyu, "Nature Writing and Ecocriticism in an Era of Posthumanism: An Interview with Scott Slovic"

Week 2: The Power of Utopia and Dystopia

  • Tao Yuanming, "Peach Blossom Spring"
  • dir. Chai Jing, Under the Dome

Week 3: What are Literature's Weaknesses?

  • Amitav Ghosh, The Great Derangement
  • Liu Cixin, "Beyond Narcissism"
  • dir. Jia Zhangke, Still Life

Week 4: What is Nature?

  • Christoph Harbsmeier, "Towards a Conceptual History of Some Concepts of Nature in Classical Chinese: ziran ziran zhili"
  • Karen Thornber, Ecoambiguity

Week 5: Debates on the Confucian Tradition

  • Chengzhou He, "New Confucianism , Science, and the Future of the Environment"
  • Tu Weiming, "The Continuity of Being: Chinese Visions of Nature"
  • Heiner Roetz, "Chinese 'Unity of Man and Nature': Reality or Myth?"
  • Selections from The Analects

Week 6: Debates on the Daoist Tradition

  • Paul Goldin, "Why Daoism is not Environmentalism"
  • Karyn Lai, "Environmental Concern: Can Humans Avoid Being Partial? Epistemological Awareness in the Zhuang Zi
  • Selections from The Dao de Jing and the Zhuangzi

Week 7: Debates on the Buddhist Tradition

  • Henrik Sørensen, "Of Eco-Buddhas and Dharma Roots: Views from the East Asian Buddhist Traditions"
  • Johan Elverskog, The Buddha's Footprint

Week 8: Poetry and the Environment

  • Corey Byrnes, Fixing Landscapes
  • Selected Poetries by Du Fu

Week 9: Was Maoism Bad for Environmental Ethics?

  • Judith Shapiro, Mao's War on Nature, selections
  • Sigrid Schmalzer, Red Revolution, Green Revolution, selections
  • dir. Li Wenhua, Breaking with the Old Ideas

Week 10: Seeking a New Ethics in the Post-Mao Era

  • Ye Yonglie, Little Smarty Travels to the Future, comic edition translated by Pan Caiying, Du Jiangguo, and Mao Yongkun
  • Ah Cheng, The King of Trees

Week 11: Roots Seeking Romanticism More than Symbolism?

  • Shen Congwen, Bordertown
  • Mark Leenhouts, "Culture Against Politics: Roots-Seeking Literature"

Week 12: Can Aesthetics Guide Us To Better Environmental Ethics?

  • Li Zehou, selected readings
  • Cao Xueqin, Dream of the Red Chamber, selected passages
  • Hao Jingfang, Folding Beijing
  • Chen Qiufan, The Fish of Lijiang

Week 13: Globalization and Waste Management

  • dir. Jiu Liangwang, Plastic China
  • Chen Qiufan, Waste Tide

Week 14: The Future of Work and Urbanization

  • Hao Jingfang, Folding Beijing
  • Chen Qiufan, The Fish of Lijiang
  • Chen Qiufan, Waste Tide, continued

Week 15: Planetary Crisis and Apocalyptic Narratives

  • Tsering Dundrop, "The Story of the Moon"
  • Liu Cixin, "The Wandering Earth"
  • dir. Frant Gwo, "The Wandering Earth"
